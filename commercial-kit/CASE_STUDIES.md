# Jiseek Commercial Case Study Framework

Purpose: turn past and future work into concise evidence that helps a senior buyer understand the problem, intervention and business value without overstating claims.

## Flagship Case Study 1 — Public Sector / Infrastructure

### Working title
From AI curiosity to operational use cases

### Situation
A public-sector or infrastructure team is exploring GenAI but needs to connect experimentation to real operational work, governance and workforce readiness.

### Business problem
- AI activity is fragmented across teams.
- Leadership lacks a prioritised view of where AI can materially improve work.
- Operational, governance and adoption constraints must be considered together.

### Work examined
Examples: reporting, knowledge retrieval, operational planning, incident workflows, stakeholder communication, frontline productivity, document-heavy processes.

### Jiseek intervention
- Executive / HOD discovery
- Workflow mapping
- AI use-case identification
- Value / feasibility / readiness / risk / workforce-impact scoring
- Role-relevant capability building
- 90-day roadmap

### Evidence to show
Use only substantiated evidence:
- number of workflows reviewed
- number of use cases identified and prioritised
- before / after workflow steps
- estimated or measured time saved
- adoption or participation rates
- decision quality / turnaround improvements
- governance or implementation decisions enabled

### Commercial takeaway
Jiseek helps operational organisations move from broad AI interest to a prioritised transformation agenda grounded in real work.

## Flagship Case Study 2 — Enterprise GenAI / Knowledge Work

### Working title
Turning individual AI usage into repeatable team productivity

### Situation
Employees already have access to AI tools, but usage is inconsistent and largely task-by-task.

### Business problem
- No common view of high-value workflows.
- Productivity gains are anecdotal rather than measured.
- Good practices are not yet embedded into repeatable ways of working.

### Jiseek intervention
- Identify recurring knowledge-work patterns
- Redesign selected workflows
- Develop practical AI patterns and guardrails
- Build team capability around the redesigned work
- Establish baseline and post-change measurements

### Evidence to show
- cycle-time reduction
- time saved per workflow
- reduction in manual steps
- quality checks / rework reduction
- adoption rate
- employee confidence / capability change

### Commercial takeaway
Jiseek moves AI from isolated personal productivity into repeatable team workflows that can be measured and improved.

## Flagship Case Study 3 — SME / Growing Organisation

### Working title
Choosing what to automate first

### Situation
Management sees many possible AI and automation tools but has limited internal capability to decide what is worth implementing.

### Business problem
- Too many possible tools and use cases
- Limited resources
- No shared prioritisation method
- Risk of buying software before defining the work problem

### Jiseek intervention
- AI Opportunity Diagnostic
- workflow and pain-point discovery
- opportunity scoring
- rapid prototype of selected use cases
- implementation effort / owner definition
- 90-day execution roadmap

### Evidence to show
- prioritised opportunity portfolio
- estimated benefit versus effort
- prototype findings
- implementation cost / effort
- baseline and expected ROI logic
- ownership and next-step plan

### Commercial takeaway
Jiseek gives smaller organisations a disciplined way to make AI investment decisions without needing a large in-house AI team.

# Standard Case Study Format

Use this format for every published case:

1. **Situation** — what was happening?
2. **Business problem** — why did it matter?
3. **Work examined** — which workflows / decisions / roles were in scope?
4. **Intervention** — what did Jiseek actually do?
5. **What changed** — what was different after the work?
6. **Evidence** — what can be measured or substantiated?
7. **What we learned** — reusable insight for future clients.

## Proof rules
- Never invent ROI, time saved or outcome percentages.
- Distinguish learner satisfaction from business impact.
- Use client names only with permission.
- Anonymised cases are acceptable when confidentiality is needed.
- Use ranges or directional evidence only if the underlying data supports them.
- Whenever possible, capture a baseline before implementation.
